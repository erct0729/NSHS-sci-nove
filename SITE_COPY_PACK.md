**Site name:** SCI-NOVE  
**Project name:** Science in Noveleta: Observation, Verification, and Exploration  
**Short tagline:** Science Starts Here  
**Audience:** Grade 11 General Science learners, Noveleta Senior High School

## Page 1 — Home

**Main headline:** Your community. Your curiosity. Your science.  
**Intro:** Welcome to SCI-NOVE — where Grade 11 learners explore science through real questions, evidence, and everyday experiences in Noveleta and Cavite.

**The SCI-NOVE Approach**

1. **Observation:** Look for patterns and changes in community experiences, photographs, simple measurements, and trusted data.
2. **Verification:** Compare explanations with reliable evidence. Separate what was observed from what is assumed.
3. **Exploration:** Apply a scientific idea to a new situation, propose a solution, and communicate your findings.

**Suggested buttons:** Explore Learning Modules / See Local Investigations.

## Page 2 — Learning Hub

**Intro:** Short, guided science modules with accessible explanations, locally relevant examples, and quick checks for understanding.

**Module 01 — Climate Change and Living Things:** Climate change refers to long-term changes in the usual patterns of temperature, rainfall, and other aspects of climate. One weather event is not enough to establish a long-term trend. Examine multiple reliable records and explore possible consequences for habitats and organisms.

**Module 02 — Chemical Reactions in Daily Life:** Chemical reactions rearrange atoms into substances with new properties. Familiar examples include acid–carbonate reactions and combustion. Never mix household cleaning products or conduct flame-based demonstrations without school-approved supervision.

**Module 03 — Ecosystems and Biodiversity:** An ecosystem includes organisms and their nonliving environment. Coastal mangrove habitats can illustrate food webs, shelter, environmental factors, and cause-and-effect predictions.

**Suggested learning page pattern:** essential question → concept explanation → local scenario → small evidence table → reflection prompt → lesson completion.

## Page 3 — Investigations

**Intro:** Explore safe, teacher-guided inquiry missions based on realistic community questions from Noveleta and nearby Cavite settings.

1. **Where Does the Rainwater Go?** Sketch or examine teacher-approved photos of school surfaces, slopes, and drains; distinguish an observation from a general claim about flood trends.
2. **Mangroves and Coastal Life:** Use credible photographs and information to make a testable prediction about habitat changes and food-web relationships.
3. **Shade, Surfaces, and Heat:** Under supervision, compare safe shaded and sunlit school surfaces with measurements, units, times, and repeated trials.
4. **What Makes the Bubbles?** Examine a teacher-approved acid–carbonate reaction demonstration; explain gas production and compare it with a physical change.

**For each investigation:** driving question → materials → Observe → Verify → Explore → expected output → safety limitations. Use the downloadable field-note template in the working prototype.

## Page 4 — Challenge Lab

**Intro:** Show how you reason with evidence, not just what you remember.

Use the included 5-item sample in the website prototype or the embeddable Google Sites quiz widget. For class-wide grading, create an accessible Google Forms quiz using the scenario-based questions and carefully review curriculum alignment, option plausibility, feedback, and learner privacy.

**Sample evidence check:** “It rained heavily yesterday, so climate change has been proven in our town.” Ask: What additional data would be needed to evaluate this statement?

## Page 5 — About SCI-NOVE

**Project description:** Science in Noveleta: Observation, Verification, and Exploration is a localized digital Science learning system designed as a pilot for Noveleta Senior High School. It encourages learners to connect General Science concepts to environmental, health, energy, and everyday community questions through guided observation, evidence verification, and exploration.

**Learning principle:** The community is a source of questions. Evidence helps us answer them.

**For teachers:** Start with a short lesson. Select a safe guided inquiry. Gather learner explanations using approved school tools. Review assessment feedback and refine the next lesson.

**Safety and privacy:** Local situations in the prototype are illustrative. Verify data before publishing local claims. Obtain approval for field activities and follow school data-privacy practices. The prototype does not have learner login, central grade storage, or automatic teacher reporting.
