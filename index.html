Module 01 / Climate & environment

Climate Change and Living Things

Understand long-term climate patterns, evaluate evidence, and explore how changing conditions can affect living organisms.

20–25 minute sample lesson

What is climate change?

Climate change refers to long-term changes in the usual patterns of temperature, rainfall, and other aspects of climate. Weather is what happens over hours or days; climate describes patterns over much longer periods.

Scientific distinction: A single rainy day, flood, or heat wave does not by itself demonstrate climate change. Researchers examine patterns and evidence over many years.

Look for the evidence

Scientists compare temperature records, precipitation patterns, ocean measurements, glacier observations, and sea-level records. Multiple independent lines of evidence provide a stronger explanation than one isolated event.

Local connection: A coastal community

Imagine learners at Noveleta Senior High School comparing long-term coastal or rainfall records from a trusted agency with photographs and local accounts. Which observations are useful starting points? Which claims need longer datasets before they can be verified?

Observation or statementWhat it tells us
There was a heavy rainfall event last week.Evidence about short-term weather; not enough alone for climate trends.
Average annual temperature data show a sustained trend across decades.Potential climate evidence, if the data are valid and methods are sound.
A resident remembers hotter summers.A useful question or lead; verify against long-term measurements.

What happens to living things?

Changes in heat, rainfall, ocean temperature, and water availability can affect habitats, growth, survival, and interactions among species. A coral-reef case, for example, can be used to explore how prolonged marine heat stress may trigger coral bleaching.

Quick check: Which is stronger climate evidence?

Choose the statement that is most directly useful for investigating a long-term change in climate.

Apply your understanding

Write a three-sentence response: (1) a local observation worth investigating, (2) the kind of evidence you would need to verify it, and (3) a possible effect on organisms if a long-term environmental change is confirmed.

Responsible use of information: If you include local climate or environmental figures, cite the agency, unit, date range, and source. No real measurements are supplied in this prototype.

Lesson checklist

  • Explain weather versus climate
  • Recognize long-term climate evidence
  • Connect environmental conditions and organisms
  • Explain a local inquiry in three sentences

Module 02 / Chemistry

Chemical Reactions in Daily Life

Use evidence of chemical change to explain common materials and safe everyday examples.

20-minute sample lesson

When is a chemical reaction happening?

A chemical reaction rearranges atoms to form substances with different properties. Observable signs can include gas formation, temperature change, color change, or a solid forming, but observations should be interpreted carefully.

Local connection: Cleaning and cooking

In familiar household scenarios, vinegar (an acid) can react with a carbonate such as eggshell material or baking soda, releasing carbon dioxide. Combustion in an LPG stove is another chemical reaction, but it must never be used as an unsupervised learner experiment.

ExampleScientific explanation
Vinegar mixed with baking soda produces bubbles.An acid–carbonate reaction releases carbon dioxide gas.
Ice melts in a bowl.A physical change of state, not a new substance.
LPG burns during cooking.Combustion involves fuel reacting with oxygen and releasing energy.
Safety: Never mix household cleaning products, conduct flame experiments, taste chemicals, or seal a gas-producing reaction in a container. Only carry out activities approved and supervised by your teacher.

Think like a scientist

If you observe bubbles during a safe, teacher-approved demonstration, explain why they might be evidence of gas production. Then name another observation or test that could strengthen your conclusion.

Reflection

Why does melting ice not count as a chemical reaction even though the material changes appearance?

Use the key idea: a physical change preserves the substance's chemical identity.

Lesson checklist

  • Describe a chemical reaction
  • Separate physical and chemical changes
  • Interpret evidence of gas formation
  • Practice safe chemical handling

Module 03 / Living systems

Ecosystems and Biodiversity

Explore how organisms interact with habitats, energy sources, and environmental changes.

20-minute sample lesson

What makes an ecosystem?

An ecosystem includes living organisms and nonliving components interacting in a particular environment. Energy flows through food chains and food webs; matter cycles through the system.

Local connection: Coastal habitats

Mangrove ecosystems are a useful example for studying coastal food webs, shelter for organisms, sediment, tides, and habitat changes. Learners can begin with trusted photos or documents before planning any supervised field observation.

Cause-and-effect investigation

Suppose a coastal habitat loses plant cover. Predict possible changes in the availability of shelter, food, and suitable breeding spaces. Label each prediction as a hypothesis that still requires evidence.

ComponentExamples to investigate
Biotic (living)Mangroves, crabs, fish, decomposers
Abiotic (nonliving)Sunlight, salinity, sediment, water movement
InteractionsFeeding, shelter, competition, nutrient cycling

Make a model

Draw a four-part cause-and-effect diagram: change in habitat condition → effect on one organism → effect on an interaction → evidence needed to test your claim.

Field safety: Use photographs or teacher-approved school materials. Do not enter waterways, disturb wildlife, or collect organisms without authorization and supervision.

Lesson checklist

  • Identify biotic and abiotic components
  • Explain ecosystem interactions
  • Predict an environmental effect
  • Distinguish a hypothesis from evidence

About the innovation

Meet Project SCI-NOVE

Science in Noveleta: Observation, Verification, and Exploration — a localized digital Science learning system designed as a pilot for Noveleta Senior High School.

Our learning principle

The community is a source of questions. Evidence helps us answer them.

SCI-NOVE encourages learners to connect Grade 11 General Science concepts to environmental, health, energy, and everyday community concerns. These connections are treated as questions for scientific investigation, not unsupported claims.

ObservationLook closely, describe patterns, and ask questions.
VerificationCheck sources, methods, measurements, and assumptions.
ExplorationApply scientific understanding in a new context.

Who is it for?

Designed first for Grade 11 General Science learners and their teachers. Additional grades, competencies, and local datasets may be added through teacher-led evaluation.

What is included in this pilot?

Three sample content modules, four guided investigation missions, a five-question practice challenge, and simple browser-based completion indicators.

How can teachers use it?

  1. Start with a short module in class or as independent review.
  2. Ask groups to choose a safe, evidence-based local investigation.
  3. Collect written reasoning using a class-appropriate tool.
  4. Use assessment results and feedback for the next lesson.

Safety and data ethics

All scenarios are illustrative. Teachers should verify current scientific data before presenting local measurements, follow school safety rules, and secure necessary permission for fieldwork. Do not upload sensitive learner information to a public site.

Website and privacy note

This is a standalone educational prototype, not an official school website or an integrated school record system. Progress saves locally in the device's browser, with no user accounts, automatic submissions, analytics, or external data collection.

Want this on Google Sites?

Build five native Google Sites pages — Home, Learning Hub, Investigations, Challenge Lab, and About — then copy the text and layouts from this prototype. For functioning interactive exercises, embed an externally hosted page or recreate assessments as Google Forms. The package includes a step-by-step implementation guide.
